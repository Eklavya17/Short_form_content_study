# RQ3: feed clustering study

Does showing same-topic videos back to back keep people watching or tire them out,
and after a like, should the next video be more of the same or something different?
Each participant scrolls three feeds that differ only in how they pick the next topic:

| Feed | Picks the next topic by | A like or a long watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Random topic not played in the last 3 videos | Does nothing |
| B | Stay or switch, by chance | Makes staying **more** likely |
| C | Stay or switch, by chance | Makes staying **less** likely |

The full design and its decisions live in the RQ3 Build Plan doc. This file is the
map of the code. RQ3 lives on the `rq3` branch; the RQ1 pilot on `main` is untouched.

## The clip catalog

`python -m rq3.build_catalog` rebuilds `data/rq3_catalog.json` and prints every clip
that needs a person to check it. Sources, in order:

1. `utils/data_with_cloudflare.csv`: topic, structure, and stage times from the
   Annotation column. Loop times come from individually timed `LOOP n` spans in the
   annotation, else from Pause Times. Query Ranges is not used because it drops each
   clip's final loop that runs to the end.
2. The MP4 header on R2: exact duration, and whether the file is faststart.
3. `data/rq3_manual_fixes.json`: hand-marked `stages` and/or `loops` that replace
   the parsed ones for a clip.

To fix a clip: watch it, edit or add its entry in the fixes file, set
`"confirmed": true`, and rebuild. Entries pre-filled by a script are best guesses
and stay on the review list until confirmed.

## Scoring a swipe

`rq3.scoring.score_outcome` returns e = (w − w̄) + g for the video that just ended:
w is the fraction watched (capped at 1), w̄ the viewer's average in this block
(0.5 for the first 3 videos), and g comes from the first rule that matches:

| # | Where they swiped | g |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The video played to its end at least once | +0.2 |
| 2 | During the hook | −0.4 |
| 3 | After the primary payoff started (`payoff_grace_s`, default 0) | +0.2 |
| 4 | Within 1 s after a loop's micro-payoff (`micro_payoff_window_s`) | +0.1 |
| 5 | Inside an annotated buildup / setup / development span | −0.1 / −0.3 / −0.2 |
| 6 | In the middle of a loop | −0.2 |
| 7 | In a gap of at most 2 s after a stage: as that stage | |
| 8 | Nothing annotated at that time | −0.2 |

Annotated stages come before loops because Pause Times often starts loop 1 at
0:00, over the hook and setup. All values are in `rq3/config.py`. Likes are not
part of e; the feed engine applies them. `classify_swipe` returns which rule
matched, which the analysis reuses as the swipe stage.

## The feed engine

All three feeds live in `rq3/feeds.py` and share everything except two rules:

| Feed | Stay on the topic? | p at a run's start | p after each video | Topic on a switch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | Never | (no p) | (no p) | Random among topics with cooldown 0 |
| B | Random draw against p | 0.75 | p + 0.3·e + 0.25·like | Softmax over topic scores |
| C | Random draw against p | 0.45 | p − 0.3·\|e\| − 0.25·like | Softmax over topic scores |

p is kept between 0.15 and 0.9 and goes back to the feed's starting value after
every switch. The different starts were adopted from the simulator results below.

`new_state` starts a block (passing clips seen in earlier blocks), `first_video`
opens it, and `choose_next` takes the viewer's `Outcome` and returns a `Decision`
with p, the draw, stay / switch / forced switch, and a JSON-safe state snapshot.
`FeedState.snapshot()` and `restore_state()` save and reload the state between
requests. Within a topic the clip is random for now; it becomes "most similar to
the last clip" once VCA similarity exists.

## Simulator results (Nov 2026)

`python -m rq3.simulation` runs 1,000 simulated participants through A, B and C
(random order, one shared pool, no repeats) for each viewer type, and prints the
three checks. Flags: `--step`, `--like-step`, `--b-start`, `--c-start`, `--videos-per-block`,
`--pool-copies N` (pretend each clip exists N times), `--run-effect`.

First run, with the 44 clips and a shared starting p (blocks of 12, step 0.3, both start at 0.6):

| Check | Result | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. B's runs at least 1 video longer than C's | +0.21 | Fail |
| 2. Analysis recovers programmed tiring of -0.10 | -0.08 | Pass |
| 3. Under 5% of switches forced by an empty topic | 30% (B) | Fail |

- **Forced switches are a pool-size problem.** At about 11 clips per topic (~88
  clips) they fall to about 3% with blocks of 12 and under 1% with blocks of 8.
- **The B-C gap tops out near +0.5 under the current rules**, whatever the step
  size, because an indifferent viewer's e averages about 0, so B's p falls as
  often as it rises.
- **Different starting p per feed closes it:** B resets to 0.75 and C to 0.45 on
  ~11 clips per topic gives a gap of +1.06, 4% forced, tiring still recovered.
  Letting B ignore below-average videos gives +0.76.

**Adopted: B starts at 0.75, C at 0.45.** Re-run with blocks of 12:

| Check | 44 clips | ~88 clips (`--pool-copies 2`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. B's runs at least 1 video longer than C's | +0.73, fail | +1.07, pass |
| 2. Recovers programmed tiring of -0.10 | -0.08, pass | -0.09, pass |
| 3. Under 5% of switches forced by an empty topic | 39%, fail | 4.7%, pass |

All three checks pass once the pool reaches about 11 clips per topic.

## Local demo (testing only)

```bash
.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m rq3.demo
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:5001 (not `localhost`, which adds ~0.3 s per request on
Windows; the page redirects there anyway). Scroll down on the video, press ↓, or
click ↓ to move on; one scroll gesture is one swipe, and scrolling up does nothing.
Pick feed A, B or C, set topic interest, press
**Start block**, and scroll. The debug panel shows, for every swipe: the scoring
rule that would match right now, how the last swipe was scored (w, w̄, g, e), why
the next video was picked (p, the draw, stay/switch/forced, topic odds or feed A's
cooldowns), topic scores, and the block history. **Download JSON** saves the block.
State lives in memory per browser; nothing is stored. `--host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000`
serves it to other machines, e.g. from a course VM.

## Conventions

- Numbers that might be tuned go in `rq3/config.py`, never inline.
- One function per rule, named for what it does; a docstring says the rule in words.
- Type hints on every function; dataclasses for records passed between modules.
- Store raw facts in the database; compute scores at analysis time.
- Unbuilt functions raise `NotImplementedError` naming the step that builds them.
